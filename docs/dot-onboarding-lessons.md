# Customer onboarding lessons

Reviewed 2026-10-09 · Public examples only

These lessons apply to Decision > Prediction onboarding and report review. They supplement the [setup manual](Decision-Prediction-onboard-setup.md) and [shared platform requirements](platforms/common.md). They do not claim a new integration or a complete historical report audit.

## Successful calculations do not prove a complete report

A report can retain correct, reproducible Python results while omitting required analytical perspectives or research sections. A prior report review identified this distinction: the numerical subset had been applied, while the required original eight-lens commentary was absent. Adding commentary repairs that omission; it does not establish that strategic frameworks, formal Bayesian updates, seeded simulation, options, metrology or the exact report anatomy were also completed.

**Practice:** read the entire skill and report anatomy before writing. Check every required section, name omissions and their consequences, and label incomplete work as a partial report. A green formula test or an added heading is not a content-completeness test.

## Make the available lenses visible at first use

Users should not have to know the playbook filenames to request them. After verifying file availability, introduce the eight original perspectives plus Scott Page, explain their purposes and ask which should lead. Guide discovery itself must be tested: documentation present in a source repository may be absent from a staged installation.

**Practice:** link onboarding from the entry documents, test the new-user path, and distinguish source availability from installation and runtime execution.

## Separate style from risk

Gerstner/Laffont analysis with Thorp-inspired survival checks is a valid combination. Buffett/Munger analysis is another preference. Neither choice determines a suitable allocation, permits leverage or changes verified facts.

**Practice:** ask about leading analysis lenses separately from time horizon, liquidity, concentration and leverage constraints. Preferred lenses may lead the summary; unfavorable evidence and full-report dissent remain visible. Explicit single-lens answers are focused views, not claims of full committee coverage.

## Separate simulated commentary from executable methods

The repository’s eight original narrative cards are not eight independently executed models. Page has a narrative card and an upstream executable pack, but the reviewed adapter does not expose arbitrary pack execution.

**Practice:** label simulated perspectives, avoid invented quotations, retain actual engine outputs unchanged, and name only the calculations that really ran. Do not turn narrative agreement into a vote count, score or probability without a defined and executed method.

## Confirmation is more than a flag

An observed screenshot transcription can be wrong, stale or unconfirmed. The skill requires genuine confirmation of exact screenshot-derived values before treating them as Measured. A legacy caveat or manually set `user_verified` flag does not create that confirmation.

**Practice:** keep unconfirmed transcription visibly separate. Preserve the private extraction identity and confirmation reference. Formal Bayesian updates additionally require a matching thesis sensitivity rule, explicit prior/likelihood assumptions, bounded updates and an actual calculation. Otherwise describe qualitative belief revision.

## Keep the four labels and the original records

Measured, Calculated, Modeled and Forecasted communicate different kinds of evidence. A Python calculation over hypothetical scenarios remains Modeled. A market price is an observation; its implied event expectation is not automatically a calibrated stock forecast.

**Practice:** label individual figures, retain engine provenance and unavailable fields, and separate supplemental calculations from original engine results. Record source/as-of, retrieval and review times. Do not mix old portfolio balances with a current quote and call the account live.

## Repair without rewriting history

A correction should say what changed and what did not. A commentary-only repair must not silently change source cutoff, numbers, assumptions or calculation records.

**Practice:** preserve the prior version, compare numerical/audit content, add a dated change entry and identify remaining gaps. Do not label commentary repairs as new engine runs, current-data refreshes or complete-specification certification.

## Optional schedules need real setup

A daily five-stock rotation, weekly chosen-ticker refresh and macro review can be useful after an on-demand example. They are customer choices, not installation defaults. No guide or adapter invocation proves a scheduler is enabled.

**Practice:** agree scope, time zone, sources, destination and private-data use; verify the host configuration and later the actual run. Preserve the same report identity with dated versions. Source failures remain visible. Research schedules do not authorize trades or external sharing.

## Verify present capabilities and costs

An earlier successful Linux test is not proof that every host, VM, VPS or operating system works. Historical model prices and cache discounts can also become stale.

**Practice:** test the chosen environment and complete reviewed bundle with synthetic inputs. Distinguish measured usage from estimates, verify current provider pricing when estimating, and mark unavailable measurements honestly. Packaging and first-use discovery need their own acceptance tests.

## Keep customer learning private when it should be

A public learning appendix should describe reproducible process lessons. Individual account data, screenshot identifiers, balances, credentials and private reports do not belong in it.

**Practice:** use public or synthetic examples in documentation. Keep a separately authorized decision diary containing the original view, evidence, assumptions, dissent, action/no action and later outcome. Preserve the original reasoning rather than rewriting it with hindsight.
